My car is much, much smarter than the public toilets in the train station, and these toilets are in turn much, much smarter than my home. And I think I understand why now.

Following the oil shocks of the 70s, US carmakers were ordered to improve the fuel efficiency of new cars by at least 40%. Congress passed this order, which the unelected president Gerald Ford felt unable to veto.

Car makers were told what results to achieve, not how to get there. This resulted in the introduction of electronics in new cars to regulate fuel injection, among other things. The computer chips had a first foothold in the car, which they would never give up.

Congress told the carmakers what to achieve, and cars became computerized. Building regulators, on the other hand, never told contractors what to aim for.
