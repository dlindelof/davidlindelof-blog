I think it is fair to say that anyone involved in research or development has, at some point in their career, been frustrated by the technology or the tools used in their organizations. I've been through it myself, though I confess that during my ill-advised youth I actively militated in favor of Windows on the workstations used by the Geneva Observatory where I carried out my master's project.

Bringing about change is no easy task. There are several forces involved, and it is a rare organization that will judge a technical idea on its technical merit alone.

In this book, Terrence Ryan has collected techniques and strategies for effectively bringing about technical change in any kind of organization. He begins his book by enumerating what he calls "Skeptics Patterns", but don't worry, this is not Yet Another Patterns Book. People opposed to technical change, according to Ryan, tend to fall into one of a limited number of categories. He gives memorable and descriptive names to each of these categories, such as "The Burned", "The Irrational", etc.

Knowing what kind of skeptic you will face makes it easy to select the appropriate technique against them (or rather, against their objections). For example, the "Create Trust" technique is deemed particularly effective against "The Burned", "The Cynic" and "The Irrational". The second third of the book is a list of such techniques, along with a list of which skeptics is will be most effective against.

The book concludes with a section on general strategies.

I enjoyed this book; Ryan has obviously given a great deal of thought into distilling his experiences into a set of easily digestible, mind-sized bites. His list of Skeptics Patterns alone is worth the price of the book; and the book includes several war stories showing how some of these techniques have been applied in real-world situations.

In short, good book, good content.
