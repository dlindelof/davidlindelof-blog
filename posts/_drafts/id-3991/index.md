I've been unemployed twice during my professional life: in January 2003, right before starting on my PhD at EPFL; and for 5 months from February to July 2018 after Neurobat filed for insolvency. Here are some tips you might find useful if you should find yourself in the same situation.

1. Play with the system
2. Weigh the pros and cons of temporary work.

The unemployment insurance system in Switzerland is excellent, probably one of the best in the world. But you need to understand what happens if you find temporary work (for example, consulting or contractual).

The unemployment office will cover a ceratin revenue for you, called the " gain assur'e" (GA). But they don't pay you the GA, but 80% of it (or 70% if you have no dependent children).

You must declare any extra income during your unemployment, which is then substracted from your GA before applying the 80%. So in the absence of any GI, you will be paid a gross

0.8 x GA

but with a GI, that amount will be 

GI + 0.8 x (GA - GI)

Rearranging the terms, this becomes

0.2 x GI + 0.8 x GA

So when you earn an extra income, be aware that you will only effectively keep for yourself 20% of what your employer pays you.

It sounds like a bummer and it is. But read on for why you would still be advised to accept temporary work.

3\. Use temporary work to your advantage.

On paper, you have the obligation to accept temporary work. But we' ve just seen that temporary work is financially not very attractive. So besides the legal obligation, why should you accept temporary work?\\It may sound trite, but I would not underestimate the value of expanding your network and keeping your skills sharop. Here are some ways that I benefitted from the two assignments I took during the Dark Days:

- Caught up with Java 8
- Learned Angular
- Learned Typescript (training on CodeSchool paid for by employer)
- By word of mouth, was considered and interviewed for a software engineering role in a fast gworing company in Geneva
- Learned to use the tidyverse set of T packages on a set of building simulation data

And let's not forget:

- renewed contact with a former colleague, who ended up hiring one of my former reports
- was able to learn of job opportunities at EPFL that I could pass on to some of my former reports
