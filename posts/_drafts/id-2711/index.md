title: "Breaking news: your team members are human"
draft: true
---

Among the great writer on software engineering, Frederick P. Brooks will always deserve my special esteem. He is perhaps best-known for his Mythical Man-Month collection of essays. But that is not why I admire him so.

He is, to the best of my knowledge, the only thought-leader to take explicitly into account our nature as fallen creatures. He must be applauded for putting his hard-earned credentials on the line by stating that the continued popularity of the Waterfall Model is the product of humanity's sinful nature.

You will fail as a team leader if you do not take into account the nature of your team members. Left to themselves, anyone will quickly fall prey to their pride, greed and sloth.

One benefit of human society is the set of rules of conduct that we deem acceptable. There are behaviors that we simply do not wish to be seen doing. One (among many) benefits of the daily scrum is that, under the guidance of a capable ScrumMaster, it becomes difficult to cover up one's own lack of progress. Luke 8:17 "For nothing is hidden that will not become evident, nor anything secret that will not be known and come to light."

Pride is a sin too, but one that can actually be used for good. Mike Cohn tells the story that he once was so proud of his own work that he printed out his code and taped it to his fridge, next to his children's drawings. The team should be encouraged to produce what Mike Cohn calls fridge-worthy work.
