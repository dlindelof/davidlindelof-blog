---
title: "Consider letting the PO sit down"
draft: true
---

Recently, I was debating with our ScrumMaster what it was that I could do to help the team in their daily work and I was pleased by the frankness of his reply: I could, he said, be more demanding of the team; more assertive.

It's a topic we had been discussing on-and-off for the past few months so it didn't come completely as a surprise. Indeed, a common complaint I hear from the team members during our one-on-ones is that one of the company's product owners sounds very much like he doesn't really care about the product. We will sometimes announce the discovery of a serious bug; or an unexpected delay; or something else that would normally greatly upset any product owner. But more often than not, such news are met with a "hmmpf" and not much more.

Some authors have argued that the PO is also the "single wringable neck" for any product. There are some problems with this point of view and many other authors disagree with it, but I think it's a good idea if the PO should at least _act_ as if his neck, and nobody else's, was on the chopping block. If you are the PO on the team, consider doing the following:

1. Show that you care about the product
2. Show that you expect exceptional high performance from the team and nothing less
3. Make the effort to remember what was said on yesterday's daily scrum

That last point is perhaps the hardest, but is also the best way (in my experience) to root out any team member that may be hampered by any impediment and is unwilling, or even afraid, to say so. I have started taking notes during the daily scrum; not in order to catch people when they do something wrong, but to react quickly when it's clear that someone is merely repeating the same thing from one day to the next.
