According to LinkedIn I've spent now four years at Neurobat. Behind my desk, on an wooden shelf, sit four moleskine notebooks dating back to the 16th August 2010. They contain dated (and in some cases, timestamped) entries, pasted graphics, computer printouts, mind-maps, meeting minutes, notes from daily scrums etc.

Except for extremely rare occasions, I never opened them again.

Once upon a time I was supposed to become a scientist. As part of our training we were taught the importance of keeping notes. The [scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method) and all that, you know. Five minutes of googling even turned up the [One True Way of Note Taking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Notes) for scientists. It's something all of us scientists and engineers are supposed to do, but do we really ever again refer to those notes? Except for a few special occasions (such as needing to report on our monthly activities) I found that I very, very rarely do.

On the other hand I would not give up my note-taking. I strongly suspect that writing things down has another unexpected benefit. It simply is an instance of accidental [spaced repetition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaced_repetition); in other words, having to reformulate in long-hand writing something that only existed in my mind reinforces that concept in my mind, making it easier for me to remember it afterwards.

It's ironic---but writing things down fixes the information in my mind, obviating the need to refer to my notes.
