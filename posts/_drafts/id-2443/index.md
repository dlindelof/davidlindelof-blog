Many years ago I used to be scientist. Then I became a programmer. I learned about such things as relational databases; Makefiles; version control systems; unit tests; etc. And hardly a day went by without me telling myself, "Gosh I wish I had known about those things when I was doing science".

Scientists work with computers. They collect, organize and store data. They write computer code to analyze that data, produce tables, summary statistics, and plots. They formulate hypotheses, and try to invalidate them. They write articles.

Programmers work with computers. They know how to organize and store data. They know how to write computer code that will be easy to understand and to modify. They know how to manage dependencies between software artefacts and source code. They try hard to prove that their code is wrong.

There is a lot of overlap between what a scientist does and what a programmer does. In fact, I will submit that the most effective scientists will be the ones who have a solid grounding in software engineering.
