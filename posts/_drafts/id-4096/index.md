I studies Physics at EPFL from 1993 to 2000. I could be mistaken, but I can't remember taking a Statistics class. I suspect that many scientists lack a formal grounding in Statistics. Around 2010 I read Jayne's excellent Probability Theory, which revealed to me that contrary to what I had thought, Statistics was the subject of much controversy, especially on some of the ponts deemed mnost essential to good data analysis. In this post I'll give you three examples, three rules that I've been following religiously over the years, unaware that they might be wrong:

1. Using N-1 as denominator when estimating sample variance
2. Biased estimators can sometimes be better
3. p-values
