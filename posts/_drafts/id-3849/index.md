I recently completed the audio version of "Failure Is Not An Option" by Gene Kranz, who played an instrumental role during many of the american space program and was flight controller during the ill-fated Apollo 13 mission.

That book has completely dispelled a notion I used to unconciously have, namely that the lunar space program was conducted in some form of waterfall: analysis followed by requirements followed by implementation followed by success.

Instead, the book tells the story of almost all space missions conducted by NASA, many of which were either failures or near-miss disasters.

I cannot help but see in the list of missions a form of incremental delivery cherished by agile advocates. Each mission appears to have been planned in order to find answers to specific questions, one at a time. For example, the first flights had as objective to first send a man in space. Next to send a man in orbit. Next to have two spacecraft rendez-vous and dock. And so on.

It's not clear from the book how the missions were planned; I would love to find a reference book that would tell that part of the story. But I suspect that many of the things we hold for granted and call "agile" were in fact understood a very long time ago.
