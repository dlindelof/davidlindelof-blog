title: "The hardest and most important question"

---

In "Exploring Requirements: Quality Before Design" Gerald Weinberg and XX give a list of what they call context-free questions. These are questions that can be asked in any project to help the requirements definition process.

By far the hardest, and yet most important question on that list is the following:

> Who is the client?

They carefully distinguish the user from the customer: the user is anyone who will affect the design of the product/project. The customer is whoever pays for it.

But when you develop a product and intend to sell it to someone who is not the final user, things get much more complicated. We make this add-on device for heating controllers, but we don't install it ourselves. A qualified electrician, trained in the installation of our system, is the one who buys the product from us and resells it to his customers. So who is the client in this context? Is it the person living in the house or is it the electrician?
