About 8 years ago I was propelled from a role of individual contributor to that of Chief Technology Office with Neurobat, at that time a young EPFL spin-off.

There's no shortage of books that will tell you the do's and don'ts when being promoted for a role of engineer to that of manager (The Manager's Path, for example). I've found the advice in such books to be invaluable, but there's a key piece of advice that I've never seen cast in writing. One so fundamental that I think it may have played a significant role in the trouble my team was facing during the last months before the insolvency. It is this:

Beware your attitude, for all eyes are upon you.

Now let me explain. I used to think of myself as relatively witty. You know, the team member who would make light jokes, small wordplays, etc. I had a knack for coming up with metaphors to describe complex things during meetings. But I was also the kind of guy whose body language would make it crystal clear whether I liked what I was doing or not.

Now almost overnight, I found myself managing a team of one, two, and soon six engineers. I like to think that our team overall was very succesful but I know I also made several mistaked, perhaps none as damagind as this one: to keep on behaving as if I was a team member with my jokes, my funny metaphors, my body language.

I can guarantee that when you manage other people you fall under the microscope of your directs. Your every move, tone of voice, joke, innuendos, implied meaning, will be picked up by your staff, and taken as a model.

Now you tell me: how can you ask people to push through hell and high water when their boss clearly shows that it's fine to dislike an assignment, and to loudly show it?
