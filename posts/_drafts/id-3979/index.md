I remember learning Emacs on the HP laptop running Windows 2000 I used during my time at CERN. I hadn't yet adopted Linux as my preferred operating system, and I accepted, without understanding, the wried file layout and naming convention prouced by Emacs on Windows.

I spent the next 10 years or so perfecting my knowledge of Emacs. I wrote three papers and my thesis in Emacs, and learned to love and use Emacs Speaks Statistics, at the time one of the best IDEs for using R.

I can't remember exactly when I switched almost overnight to vi. I think it must hav eeen during the early years at Neurobat, say about late 2010. Following the advice of Jeff Atwood I had learned how to touch type; like many developers, I knew at the time enough vi keybindings to perform elementary file editions, opening a file (almost always accidentally with vi), entering insert mode, exiting edit mode, saving the file, and quitting vi. When I was very lucky I could do these tasks in that order.

But when I learned to touch type, vi's keybindings suddenly began to make sense to me and I resented more and more Emacs's lack of modes, and its heavy use of the Ctrl key.

So I decided to work through vi's tutorial that ships with it and I was hooked. I haven't used Emacs since then.
