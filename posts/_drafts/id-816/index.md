I first heard about this book on the [Climate Report podcast](http://tombowman.com/). The problem with many scientists is their reluctance, or sometimes their inability, to effectively communicate their research outside of academic circles. Indeed, seeking attention from the mainstream media has traditionally been frowned upon by academia; the proper outlet to publish one's work has always been through the peer-review process.

But we live in an age where decisions must be made and policies must be adopted, and we are in desperate need of reliable scientific information to help us make those decisions. The current climate change conversation must be fueled by science, not by agendas. It is, therefore, the responsibility of all scientists to inform the general public of any findings that could have an impact on the decision-making process. This book is about helping those scientists do just that.

Perhaps the biggest value I got from this book was the concept of a "message box": this is a tool that will help scientists define their message, and improve the likelihood that it will be understood by the (busy) layman who cannot, and will not, be bothered to read several pages of an academic paper before coming to the results section.

I can strongly recommend this book to any scientist who want to have their work used intelligently.
